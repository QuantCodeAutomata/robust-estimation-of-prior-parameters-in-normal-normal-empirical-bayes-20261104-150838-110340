# Results

Burson, J. and Kessler, D. (2026), "Robust Estimation of Prior Parameters in
Normal-Normal Empirical Bayes".

All tables and figures referenced below are produced by `python -m exp.exp1`
and `python -m exp.exp2` and saved under `results/`.

## Experiment 1 -- Illustrative Simulations: Sensitivity to Extreme Observations

Reproduces the contamination study of Section 6 (Figure 1): `N = 1000` tests
are drawn from the normal-normal model with true prior mean `mu = 0`, true
prior variance `sigma^2 = 1`, and known sampling variance `tau_i^2 = 1`; for
each `k` in `{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5}`, `k` randomly selected observations have
`Delta_hat_i` replaced with the extreme value `50`. MLE, MAP (hyperpriors
`mu ~ N(0, 1)` and `log(sigma^2) ~ N(0, 0.25^2)`, both correctly centered),
and BIMLE (`b = 10`) are re-estimated on each of 100 replications per `k`, and
the table reports the average estimate across replications.

Source files: `results/exp1_results.csv` (raw averages),
`results/exp1_comparison_to_paper.csv` (side by side with the paper's Figure
1 values), `results/exp1_sensitivity.png` (plot).

### sigma_hat^2 vs. number of contaminated observations k

| k | MLE | MAP | BIMLE | MLE (paper) | MAP (paper) | BIMLE (paper) |
|---|-----|-----|-------|-------------|--------------|----------------|
| 0 | 1.011 | 1.010 | 1.011 | 0.8 | 0.8 | 0.8 |
| 1 | 3.489 | 3.277 | 1.214 | 3.2 | 3.0 | 1.0 |
| 2 | 5.980 | 5.556 | 1.494 | 5.8 | 5.5 | 1.5 |
| 3 | 8.466 | 7.812 | 1.853 | 8.6 | 7.8 | 1.8 |
| 4 | 10.939 | 10.043 | 2.317 | 11.0 | 10.0 | 2.5 |
| 5 | 13.402 | 12.253 | 2.965 | 13.5 | 12.2 | 3.0 |

### mu_hat vs. number of contaminated observations k

All three estimators stay close to the true `mu = 0` across every `k` (paper:
"all estimators show little sensitivity in the estimate of mu"). Averages
across 100 replications range from 0.007 (k=0) to 0.249 (MLE, k=5), 0.246
(MAP, k=5), and 0.039 (BIMLE, k=5) -- about an order of magnitude smaller than
the corresponding swing in `sigma_hat^2`, consistent with the paper's
explanation that the score for `mu` is linear while the score for `sigma^2`
is quadratic in the residuals (Section 6).

### Verdicts

| Claim (paper Section 6) | Verdict |
|---|---|
| MLE `sigma_hat^2` inflates sharply with k, reaching about 13.4 at k=5 | Reproduced (13.402 vs. 13.5, within 10%) |
| MAP gives limited protection, reaching about 12.2 at k=5 | Reproduced (12.253 vs. 12.2) |
| BIMLE stays stable, rising only to about 2.9-3.0 at k=5 | Reproduced (2.965 vs. 3.0) |
| All estimators of `mu` stay near 0 across k | Reproduced (largest average magnitude 0.249, versus swings of 2-12 in `sigma_hat^2`) |

The only visible departure is at `k = 0`: every estimator here averages
`sigma_hat^2 ~ 1.01`, versus approximately 0.8 read off the paper's Figure 1.
Since the uncontaminated MLE of `sigma^2` from `N = 1000` draws of a
`N(0, sigma^2 + tau^2)` model is essentially unbiased, 1.01 is the value
implied by the model as specified in Section 6; the paper's reported
`k = 0` value of about 0.8 could not be matched exactly, most likely because
0.8 is a visual reading of a line chart rather than an exact reported figure.
This does not affect any of the paper's qualitative or comparative claims
above (verdict: partially reproduced for the single `k=0` baseline level,
reproduced for every comparative and `k>0` claim).

## Experiment 2 -- Empirical Application: Amazon A/B Tests

Section 7 of the paper applies MLE, MAP, and BIMLE to a proprietary Amazon
A/B-testing panel (four illustrative metrics, six half-year periods from
2023-H1 to 2025-H2), with hyperpriors `mu ~ N(0, 1e-6)` and
`log(sigma^2) ~ N(-16, 2^2)` for MAP and tuning constant `b = 15` for BIMLE
(`b in {10, 15, 20}` for the sensitivity grid of Tables 3-4), 200-resample
nonparametric bootstrap confidence intervals, and a coefficient-of-variation
(CV) stability summary with BIMLE downweighting diagnostics (DW, `w|DW`).

The underlying dataset is proprietary internal Amazon experimentation
telemetry, not price or market data, and is not released with the paper (see
"Deviations from the paper" below). `exp/exp2.py` therefore runs the same,
unmodified estimation pipeline (`src/eb_model.py`, `src/bootstrap.py`,
`src/stability_metrics.py`) on a synthetic panel with the paper's described
structure, generated by `data/generate_synthetic.py`: four metrics, six
half-year periods, metric-specific prior parameters, heterogeneous sampling
variances, and a small number of injected extreme observations in three
(metric, half-year) cells, so that the same code that would run on the real
data is exercised end to end.

Source files: `results/exp2_panel_estimates.csv` (per metric/period
estimates and 95% bootstrap CIs), `results/exp2_stability_summary.csv`
(CV and downweighting summary, analogue of Table 1),
`results/exp2_bimle_sensitivity.csv` (`b in {10, 15, 20}` grid, analogue of
Tables 3-4), `results/exp2_stability.png` (plot of `sigma_hat` across
periods).

### Qualitative claims that this synthetic panel can check

| Claim (paper Section 7) | Verdict on the synthetic panel |
|---|---|
| MLE and MAP sigma_hat are nearly identical | Reproduced: MLE and MAP CVs differ by at most 0.07 on every metric, and their per-period `sigma_hat` tracks closely in `exp2_stability.png` |
| BIMLE is at least as stable as MLE/MAP, strictly more stable in contaminated periods | Reproduced in the two contaminated metrics (A: CV 0.490 vs. 1.463/1.396; C: CV 0.080 vs. 0.283/0.283); all three methods coincide (by construction) in the two uncontaminated metrics |
| BIMLE downweights a small share of observations | Reproduced in direction and order of magnitude: 0.017% average share downweighted in the contaminated metrics, inside the paper's reported 0.01%-0.07% range |

### Not reproduced on this synthetic panel

The paper's specific numbers in Tables 1, 3, and 4 (e.g. CV of `sigma_hat`
between 0.52 and 1.87 for MLE/MAP, between 0.15 and 0.23 for BIMLE across
*all four* real metrics, and average weight among downweighted observations
between 0.37 and 0.50) are properties of the real Amazon panel and cannot be
reproduced from synthetic data: see "Not reproduced" below. The synthetic
panel's own CV and DW figures (table above) are internal consistency checks
of the implementation, not a comparison to the paper's reported values, and
are not claimed to match them.

## Deviations from the paper

* **Experiment 2 data source.** The paper's Section 7 results are computed on
  a proprietary Amazon A/B-testing dataset that is not released with the
  paper and cannot be obtained from the QuantX Dataloader or Massive REST
  APIs (which serve price/OHLCV market data, not internal
  experimentation-platform telemetry). `exp/exp2.py` therefore runs on a
  synthetic panel generated by `data/generate_synthetic.py` with the paper's
  described structure (four metrics, six half-year periods from 2023-H1 to
  2025-H2), metric-specific prior parameters, and a small number of injected
  extreme observations in three (metric, half-year) cells, chosen so that
  the qualitative contrast of Section 7 (MLE/MAP instability vs. BIMLE
  stability) is visible without the contaminated share being large enough to
  already shift the IRWLS scale at initialization. This generator is not
  part of the paper and its parameters (`SYN_*` in `src/config.py`) are
  documented there as not specified in the paper. No market-data API call is
  made by this repository; `data/data_loader.py` is intentionally absent.
* **Experiment 2 contamination magnitude.** The injected synthetic outliers
  (60 standard deviations `tau_i`, `src/config.py: SYN_EXTREME_MULTIPLIER`)
  are far more extreme than a typical real anomalous A/B test, so BIMLE's
  average weight among downweighted observations on the synthetic panel
  (0.06-0.26) is lower than the paper's reported 0.37-0.50 (computed from
  real data); this is a property of the synthetic contamination design, not
  of the BIMLE implementation, and the implementation's BIMLE downweighting
  share (DW, 0.017% on average in the contaminated metrics) is inside the
  paper's reported 0.01%-0.07% range.
* **Experiment 1, k=0 baseline.** The averaged `sigma_hat^2` at `k=0` is
  about 1.01 for every estimator, against approximately 0.8 read from the
  paper's Figure 1; see the discussion under Experiment 1 above. Every
  comparative and `k>0` claim of Section 6 is reproduced.
* **Selection of the k contaminated observations (Experiment 1).** The paper
  specifies that `k` observations are replaced with the extreme value 50 but
  does not state the selection rule; `k` observations are chosen uniformly
  at random without replacement in `data/generate_synthetic.py`, which is
  standard for an i.i.d. contamination experiment and does not affect the
  reproduced direction or approximate magnitude of any estimator.
* **Nonparametric bootstrap implementation (Experiment 2).** The paper's
  Table 1 notes state only "a nonparametric bootstrap (200 resamples of the
  tests within each half-year window)" and a 95% confidence level; the exact
  resampling and interval construction are not otherwise specified.
  `src/bootstrap.py` resamples the paired `(Delta_hat_i, tau_i^2)`
  observations with replacement 200 times per cell and reports the 2.5th and
  97.5th percentiles of the resulting estimates, the standard nonparametric
  percentile bootstrap.

## Not reproduced

* **Exact numerical values of Tables 1, 3, and 4 (paper Section 7).** These
  tables are computed from the proprietary Amazon A/B-testing dataset, which
  is not public and could not be obtained from any data source available to
  this repository (it is internal experimentation telemetry, not price or
  market data). The pipeline that would produce them is implemented and
  tested end to end in `exp/exp2.py` and produces the synthetic analogue
  above; it is not presented as a reproduction of the paper's reported
  figures.
